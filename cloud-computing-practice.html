1 Cloud models & service delivery

📝 Practice quiz — Part 1

Six situations. Pick the best answer; every question explains all four options as soon as you choose.

Q1 · Four hospitals, one rulebook

Four regional hospitals want to share cloud infrastructure. All four answer to the same patient-privacy regulator, all four need the same audit logging, and none of them wants to fund a data center alone. They will govern the environment jointly, and no outside customer will ever use it. Which deployment model fits?

Answer: B. ✔ A community cloud is the model defined by a shared concern — common security, policy, compliance, or mission requirements — with the infrastructure governed collectively by the participating organizations and closed to everyone else. Four hospitals under one privacy regulator, splitting the cost and carrying identical audit obligations, is the textbook case. ✘ A — a public cloud is offered for open use; the hospitals would be tenants alongside anyone with a credit card, and not one of them would govern it. ✘ C — a private cloud is dedicated to a single organization, which is exactly the arrangement each hospital rejected when it decided not to fund one alone. ✘ D — "exclusive cloud" is not one of the four deployment models (public, private, community, hybrid). An option naming a model you have never studied is a distractor, not a gap in your notes.

Q2 · The browser tab that is the whole product

The sales team at Gulfstream Supply signs in to a complete customer-management application through a browser. They configure fields and run reports; the vendor owns the code, the servers, the patching, and the upgrade schedule. Which service model is this?

Answer: C. ✔ Software as a Service delivers a finished application through an interface. The customer's job stops at configuration and use, while the provider runs the application, the runtime, the servers, and the upgrades — which is why the sales team can adopt a CRM without hiring an administrator. ✘ A — with IaaS the customer receives raw compute, network, and storage, and owns the guest operating system and everything above it; nobody on this sales team installs anything. ✘ B — PaaS gives developers a managed place to deploy their own code; this team writes no code, it fills in fields. ✘ D — browser delivery is not a fourth category, it is simply how SaaS usually reaches a user.

Q3 · One application, three hundred companies

Meridian's accounting application serves roughly 300 client companies from one shared application environment. Each company sees only its own ledgers, users, and settings, and one company's configuration change never appears in another's account. Which design does this illustrate?

Answer: D. ✔ Multitenancy is the design where a single application environment serves many customers — "tenants" — while logically isolating each tenant's data and configuration. It is the economics behind SaaS: one codebase, one operations team, hundreds of customers, with the separation enforced in software instead of by giving everyone their own servers. ✘ A — failover is a reliability mechanism about what happens when something breaks, and nothing in this story has broken. ✘ B — load balancing distributes requests across resources; that is a capacity concern, not what keeps 300 companies' ledgers apart. ✘ C — SSO is about authentication across multiple applications, while this describes one application with separated data.

Q4 · Three services, one screen

Palm Coast Travel's new trip dashboard pulls maps from one service, forecasts from a second, and hotel availability from a third, then presents all three together as a single planning screen. What is the combined result called?

Answer: A. ✔ A mashup combines content or capabilities drawn from multiple services or data sources into one new experience. The value lives in the combination — not one of the three upstream services, on its own, shows a traveler a map, a forecast, and an available room in a single view. ✘ B — a merger is a business transaction between companies; the word sits close enough to "mashup" to tempt a fast reader and describes something entirely unrelated. ✘ C — a hypervisor sits between virtual machines and physical hardware, several layers below anything to do with combining web content. ✘ D — a SAN is storage infrastructure inside a data center, not an application-level combination of services.

Q5 · Moving the servers out of the closet

Bayside Marine moves its servers out of a back-office closet and into an ISP's managed data center. Which outcome is least likely to be a benefit of that move?

Answer: C. ✔ Read the question again: it asks for the non-benefit. The entire point of moving servers into a provider facility is to reduce what the company has to do at its own site, so ending up with more on-site administration back at the office would mean the move failed. ✘ A — capacity on demand is one of the standard hosting benefits; the ISP already owns racks, power, and bandwidth the closet never had. ✘ B — improved uptime is usually the headline reason: redundant power, cooling, connectivity, and staff on site around the clock. ✘ D — lower facility cost follows directly, because the company stops paying to power and cool a room full of servers. Three options are genuine benefits; the exam rewards noticing which question is actually being asked.

Q6 · The platform and the twenty-year-old system

Northshore Freight's developers choose PaaS to ship a new customer portal faster. Their risk review has to list what could still go wrong after that decision. Which concern belongs on the list?

Answer: D. ✔ Integration is the real PaaS tradeoff, alongside provider dependence and data-security questions. The platform accelerates new code, but the twenty-year-old dispatch system still has to exchange data with the portal, and the platform constrains how that connection can be built. A risk review lists what survives the decision, and this survives it. ✘ A — buying or building hardware is exactly what a cloud customer never does in any service model; that is the provider's business. ✘ B — nothing about PaaS requires open-sourcing your application; you keep ownership of your code. ✘ C — a platform with no outside network access could not serve a customer portal at all, since PaaS applications are network-facing by definition.

2 Reliability, identity, storage & voice

🎮 Sorter 3 · Keeping the service up

Redundancy vs. failover is the pair people lose points on. Redundancy is the standing spare capacity; failover is the act of moving work onto it. You can have redundancy that never fails over — and untested failover that never works.

📝 Practice quiz — Part 2

Seven situations covering availability, identity, storage, and voice.

Q7 · Four servers, one queue

A controller at Sandpiper Retail receives every incoming web request and hands each one to whichever of four healthy application servers is best placed to answer it, so no single server is buried during the lunch rush. What is it doing?

Answer: A. ✔ Load balancing is precisely this: distributing incoming work across multiple computing resources so that none becomes the bottleneck. Paired with health checks it also supports availability, because the balancer simply stops sending traffic to a server that stops answering. ✘ B — SSO is an identity mechanism; it establishes who you are, not which server answers you. ✘ C — loose coupling is a design property of the relationship between a program and a service, not a runtime routing decision. ✘ D — file synchronization keeps copies of data consistent across devices; nothing here is being synchronized, requests are being routed.

Q8 · The spares that sit ready

Cypress Insurance keeps a duplicate set of servers, network links, and power supplies standing ready so service continues if the primary set fails. Those duplicate resources are described as what?

Answer: C. ✔ Redundancy means duplicating important components so that one failure does not take the service down. Keep its companion term straight: failover is the act of switching work onto those duplicates — redundancy is the standing capacity, failover is the move. ✘ A — coupling describes how strongly one piece of software depends on another and says nothing about spare capacity. ✘ B — virtualization separates logical resources from physical hardware; a virtual machine can be a single point of failure just as easily as a physical one, so virtualizing something does not make it redundant. ✘ D — multitenancy is about serving many customers from shared infrastructure, an economics decision rather than a resilience one.

Q9 · Four letters on the sign-in page

The IT bulletin at Live Oak College announces that "SSO is now enabled for the library, the grade portal, and campus email." In identity and access management, what does SSO stand for?

Answer: D. ✔ SSO is single sign-on: a user authenticates once with a trusted identity service and then reaches every participating application without re-entering credentials. The payoff runs both ways — fewer passwords for the user, and one place for IT to disable an account the day someone leaves. ✘ A — "shared storage organization" describes nothing in identity management; the storage term from this unit is SAN. ✘ B — "secure server operation" sounds like hardening a server, an operations task with no bearing on how often a user types a password. ✘ C — "system service optimization" is performance-tuning language, and SSO removes logins, not latency. All three wrong expansions are assembled from plausible IT vocabulary, which is exactly how definitional questions are built.

Q10 · What the identity server hands over

During an SSO login at Live Oak College, the authentication server has just verified a student's password successfully. The student now clicks through to the grade portal. What should normally happen next?

Answer: A. ✔ The identity or authentication server issues a ticket or token — portable, time-limited proof that authentication already happened. The student presents it to the grade portal, the portal validates it against the trusted identity service, and access is granted with no second password prompt. That single step is the mechanism of single sign-on. ✘ B — collecting the password again is exactly what SSO exists to eliminate; if the portal asked, single sign-on would not be in use. ✘ C — deleting the identity would end the account rather than authorize it; identities persist across sessions. ✘ D — nothing about a successful login requires disabling another server, and doing so would break the access the student just earned.

Q11 · The network that only storage uses

Inside a data center, a dedicated high-speed network connects a bank of servers to pooled storage devices, kept separate from the network that carries ordinary user traffic. Which technology is being described?

Answer: B. ✔ A storage area network is a specialized, high-speed network whose only job is connecting servers to shared storage. Keeping it separate from the general-purpose network is the whole point: storage traffic is heavy and latency-sensitive, so isolating it protects performance and reliability while letting any server on the SAN reach the pooled capacity. ✘ A — SOA is a software architecture pattern, not a physical network. ✘ C — SSO is an identity mechanism and moves no data between servers and disks. ✘ D — VoIP carries voice calls; the acronym is one letter from the answer and the meaning is nowhere near it. These four are offered together on purpose.

Q12 · Files that live somewhere else

An application at Harbor Lane Books creates, reads, renames, and deletes files that physically reside in cloud storage, using ordinary file-system operations as though the files sat on a local disk. Which term best fits?

Answer: B. ✔ A cloud file system exposes cloud-resident files through familiar operations — create, read, rename, delete — so applications and users work with them the way they work with a local disk, while the bytes actually live in the provider's storage. ✘ A — a hypervisor allocates CPU, memory, and storage to virtual machines; it presents no file interface to applications. ✘ C — FIDM is federated identity management, which shares identity information across organizational boundaries: right course, wrong unit. ✘ D — network-attached storage sits on a local network, which is not what "resides in cloud storage" describes, and the word "only" narrows the claim further than the scenario supports.

Q13 · The call that never touches a phone line

A support agent at Sandpiper Retail places a customer call from a headset plugged into her laptop. The audio travels over the company's internet connection, and no traditional telephone line is involved anywhere. Which technology makes that possible?

Answer: C. ✔ Voice over Internet Protocol digitizes speech, breaks it into packets, and carries it across IP networks such as the internet. That is why a cloud phone system follows an agent to any location with connectivity, and why call features (routing, recording, queues) become software features. ✘ A — CFS is about files, not audio streams. ✘ B — PaaS is where developers deploy code; a VoIP product might run on a platform, but the platform is not what carries the call. ✘ D — a SAN moves storage traffic inside a data center and never reaches a customer's phone.

📝 Practice quiz — Part 3

Seven situations covering collaboration tools, virtualization, and service architecture.

Q14 · Publishing is not collaborating

A project team lists the tools it uses. Using this course's categories, which one is primarily a media-publishing destination rather than a tool for working together?

Answer: A. ✔ In this course's classification, a public video-sharing channel is a publishing platform: the flow runs outward, from the team to an audience, and the audience does not co-create the work. ✘ B — a blog the team writes and comments on together is listed as a collaboration tool; the co-authoring and the comment thread are the collaboration. ✘ C — instant messaging is the most direct collaboration tool of the four, existing so that people can work together in real time. ✘ D — a wiki is the classic example, where several members edit the same pages and the revision history records the collaboration. The distinction being tested is direction: broadcasting to an audience versus building with a team.

Q15 · One box that behaves like six

A single physical server at Bayfront Logistics presents itself to users as six independent servers, each running its own operating system, and any one of them can reboot without disturbing the other five. Which concept creates that illusion?

Answer: D. ✔ Virtualization abstracts physical hardware so that several isolated virtual machines, each with its own guest operating system, run on one host. The isolation is the tell in this story: one virtual server reboots and the other five never notice. ✘ A — "load delegation" is not a standard term; the real concept for spreading work is load balancing, and it does not create operating systems. ✘ B — multitenancy shares one application among customers, a different layer entirely; here there are six separate operating systems. ✘ C — visualization means charts and dashboards. It is one letter from the answer, which is exactly why it appears here.

Q16 · The layer that hands out the hardware

On that same host, a piece of specialized software decides how much physical CPU time, memory, and storage each virtual machine receives, and prevents any one virtual machine from reaching into another's memory. What is that software called?

Answer: B. ✔ The hypervisor is the layer between virtual machines and the host's physical resources: it creates and runs the VMs, schedules their access to CPU, memory, and storage, and enforces the isolation boundary between them. ✘ A — an authentication ticket belongs to the identity unit and carries proof of a login, not an allocation of hardware. ✘ C — a wiki manages pages, not processors. ✘ D — a mashup combines services at the application level, far above the hardware. Hold this alongside the previous question: virtualization is the concept, and the hypervisor is the software that implements it.

Q17 · Three of these are not like the fourth

A study group lists four products that appear in the virtualization unit's readings. Which one is best known for cloud file storage and synchronization rather than for creating or running virtual machines?

Answer: C. ✔ Dropbox is a cloud storage, synchronization, and file-sharing service: it keeps copies of files current across devices and people. It appears in the readings beside the virtualization products, which is exactly why it is worth filing separately in memory. ✘ A — Parallels Desktop runs guest operating systems on a personal computer, so a Mac can run Windows in a virtual machine. ✘ B — VMware is the best-known commercial virtualization vendor, from desktop products up to data-center hypervisors. ✘ D — Microsoft's server virtualization tooling creates and manages virtual machines on Windows Server. Three of the four build virtual machines; one moves files.

Q18 · Capabilities with contracts

Northshore Freight redesigns its software so that each business capability — rating a shipment, printing a label, tracking a package — becomes an independent service with a clearly defined interface that other applications can call and reuse. Which architecture is that?

Answer: A. ✔ Service-oriented architecture organizes software capabilities as independent, reusable, discoverable services that communicate through defined contracts. "Rating a shipment" becomes one service every application calls, instead of the same logic copied into three applications that then drift apart. ✘ B — single-server operation is a deployment fact about where software runs and says nothing about how capabilities are organized. ✘ C — "storage-oriented access" is invented; the storage term from this course is SAN. ✘ D — "software optimization association" is a plausible-sounding trade body, not an architecture. When three expansions of an acronym are unfamiliar, trust the one you have actually seen defined.

Q19 · Upgrade the service, keep the callers

Northshore's shipping service is rebuilt internally — new database, new code, faster rating engine — while its published interface stays exactly the same, so none of the eleven applications that call it needs to be rewritten. Which quality does that demonstrate?

Answer: D. ✔ Loose coupling means a caller depends on the service's published contract rather than on its internals. As long as that interface holds, the service can be rewritten, re-hosted, or re-tuned without touching a single client — which is why loose coupling is the property that makes SOA pay for itself. ✘ A — redundancy is about surviving failures, and nothing failed here. ✘ B — colocation concerns where hardware physically sits, not how software depends on software. ✘ C — tight coupling is the exact opposite, and it is the failure mode being avoided: under tight coupling, swapping the database would have forced eleven rewrites.

Q20 · What software cannot do

A vendor's catalog lists things its web services can do. Which listed request is least like an operation a web service can actually carry out on its own?

Answer: B. ✔ A web service exchanges data or performs a software operation through a defined interface. It can coordinate a delivery — price it, book it, track it, notify the customer — but the last step happens in the physical world, performed by a person or a vehicle, not by the service. ✘ A — returning weather for a ZIP code is the standard textbook example of a data-returning web service. ✘ C — an IP-to-country lookup is a pure data operation over a table the service already holds. ✘ D — accepting a submitted report is the inbound direction of the same idea: a web service can receive and store data as readily as it can return it. The boundary this question draws is between information and physical action.
